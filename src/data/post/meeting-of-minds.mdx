Meeting of Minds is a longitudinal art-science research collaboration with **NobleMotion Dance** and **Musiqa**, studying coupled brain activity, dance, and music as two dancers interact from a state of discord to collaboration, across rehearsals and public performance. The project uses brain-computer interface (BCI) and analysis techniques, including hyperscanning, adaptive noise canceling, and mobile brain-body imaging (MoBI), to capture intra- and inter-brain synchrony throughout the dance. Data is collected simultaneously via 28-channel scalp EEG, 4 channels of EOG, one IMU for head motion, and video recording, during both rehearsals and public performances.

The performance was presented at the **United Nations AI for Good Summit in Geneva** on May 31, 2024. I traveled to Geneva as part of the UH BRAIN Center team to record the mobile brain imaging data during the performance.

## The Performance

Meeting of Minds is structured in 10 scenes, each representing a different type of interaction between the dancers, Lauren Serrano and Tyler Orcutt. The first four scenes are solo performances. Scene 5 marks the first time the dancers perform together, in a state of conflict. As the choreography, by Andy and Dionne Noble, progresses, the dancers engage through eye contact, touch, and synchronized movement, culminating in a cooperative climax that integrates all of these interactions.

## Live Brain Visualization

During recordings, data is filtered and processed in real time via a BCI to drive visual projections, designed by Shepherd School doctoral candidate Badie Khaleghian, that show the level of inter-brain synchrony as it happens, making Meeting of Minds one of the first dance performances to visualize inter-brain synchrony live. A novel EEG denoising pipeline developed for this project is also in preparation for presentation.

## Presented at IUCRC BRAIN

An abstract on this work, _Neural dynamics of social interaction during creative movements from a state of discord to "Meeting of Minds": A longitudinal art-science performance_, was presented at the IUCRC BRAIN Symposium.

## Origin

Meeting of Minds began in **November 2023** as a follow-up to LiveWire, originating from a **TEDx talk at Sam Houston State University** by Andy Noble. The most recent data collection took place on **October 2, 2025**.

As part of the ongoing Meeting of Minds project, I mentored Harshil Vijayan, an NSAP student, through his summer research contribution to the study. Harshil worked hands-on with the mobile brain-body imaging (MoBI) data, structuring it and carrying out the functional mapping that lays the groundwork for the synchrony analysis ahead. He presented this work as a poster at the IUCRC BRAIN Symposium.

Alongside this, I mentored him on building a tool aimed at making brain signal processing more accessible to other researchers working with this kind of data. We are currently developing this data descriptor.
