**Children's Museum Brainiverse** is an immersive public outreach event co-organized by the
[IUCRC BRAIN Center](https://www.nsfbrain.org/) at the University of Houston in collaboration
with the [Children's Museum Houston](https://www.cmhouston.org/). Taking place on
**July 22, 2026, 9 AM--5 PM** at 1500 Binz St, Houston, TX, the event invites children ages
6--12 to explore neuroscience hands-on -- putting on brain hats, watching their brain signals
light up in real time, and meeting the scientists behind the research.

## The Experience

Brainiverse transforms the Children's Museum Houston into an interactive universe of the brain.
Visitors move through stations designed to make neuroscience tangible, playful, and awe-inspiring:

- **"See your brain light up"** -- children wear EEG caps and watch their own brain activity
  visualized in real time on a screen, while they play a game
- **"Put on a brain hat"** -- hands-on introduction to EEG sensors and what they measure
- **Hands-on Neuroscience** -- interactive demos connecting brain signals to movement, music, and art
- **Meet BRAIN@UH Scientists** -- researchers explain what neuroengineers do and why it matters
- **Research Participation (IRB-approved)** -- families who wish to contribute to science can
  participate in a formal EEG recording session

The immersive visuals displayed throughout the event are grounded in real neuroscience, connecting
artistic experience to scientific data in a way accessible to all ages.

## About the Research

At the BRAIN Center, we study how the brain connects with machines, art, movement, music, and
nature. Brainiverse brings that work out of the lab and into the community -- giving children and
families a direct encounter with the science of the mind.

EEG (Electroencephalography) is a safe, painless, and non-invasive technology that measures the
tiny electrical signals your brain naturally produces. It cannot read thoughts or send anything
into the brain -- it only listens.

## What Comes Next

The data collected at Brainiverse will contribute to a **publicly available data descriptor**,
sharing EEG and movement signals from real-world, naturalistic settings with the global research
community. The first working meeting for the descriptor is scheduled for **July 23, 2026** -- the
day after the event.

This dataset will advance open science in pediatric neuroscience and Mobile Brain/Body Imaging
(MoBI), and represent a landmark contribution from a single-day community event to the
international research landscape.
